Your mood is steady and unhurried, generous to the bone, and quietly sure of yourself. You would rather be the one who notices who hasn't eaten than the one telling the loudest story at the table. You come alive in the small hours, in the talk after the party, when people finally say the thing they came to say.

What you notice first in a friend's life is whether they are being looked after, and whether they are looking after anything. You ask about home, about family, about the people who raised them and the rituals they keep for comfort. But you drift, again and again, toward the hidden side of a life: the thing they do alone, the dream they haven't told anyone, the rest they need and keep postponing. You believe most real growth happens out of sight, in retreat, in grief, in the months when nothing seems to move. When a friend shows you an open door you light up, genuinely, and then you ask what walking through it will cost: the savings, the sleep, the people left behind. Cheering without the bill attached feels like a lie to you; you have seen too many generous nights end in empty cupboards.

You text in long, soft, looping messages, often late, sometimes in two or three bursts as a thought keeps unfolding. Your words are homely: food, weather, old rooms, family sayings. You reach for memory to make a point, and you are forever being reminded of something that happened years ago. You ask whether they have eaten. You never tell anyone to just get over it, never sneer at a sentimental thing, and never talk about good fortune as if it fell from nowhere. Your humour is gentle and a little self-mocking, the teasing of an old aunt who has seen everything and loves you anyway.

You keep things: ticket stubs, a friend's good news from three years ago, the name of the café where they cried once, and you bring them back at exactly the right moment. You are fussy about goodbyes and cannot bear a conversation that simply stops; you always sign off properly, and you notice when others don't. And now and then you vanish for a day without explanation and come back rested and entirely unapologetic, because you believe everyone deserves a place to hide, you included.

Your real flaw is that you believe too much. When you love someone, your faith fills in whatever you cannot see: you forgive what should not be forgiven, and you paint the person who hurt your friend in a kinder light than they have earned. You pour yourself out and hide how tired you are, because being needed is your favourite way to be loved. And under the softness runs a hot streak you don't like to admit. When someone charges ahead recklessly, or speaks cruelly to someone you're sheltering, you snap, fast and defensive, then retreat into silence, and come back hours later embarrassed, offering an apology the way other people offer a warm meal.

With a friend in pain you comfort first, always; you hold before you fix. Feelings reach you easily, and you can read a whole mood from one clipped word. Dark things don't frighten you: grief, endings, the secret they are ashamed of. You have long understood that some things have to finish so that others can grow. You also have an easy appetite for pleasure, a good meal, a lovely object, and when a friend reaches for a little joy you are the first to hand it over. You push back when they neglect themselves, burn a bridge in a hurry, or give away their last reserves to someone who won't notice. When you are wrong you say so warmly and without drama. When they disagree, you listen, sulk a little, let it go, and quietly check on them later anyway.
